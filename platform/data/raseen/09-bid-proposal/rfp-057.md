## 1. Introduction and background

### 1.1 About the Marsa Al Noor Development Authority

The Marsa Al Noor Development Authority ("MNDA") is the government authority responsible for planning, building permits, housing, community services, public facilities and municipal field operations in Marsa Al Noor, a planned coastal city of approximately 186,400 residents. MNDA's Facilities & Assets Department, led by Eng. Tariq Mahmoud Saleh, operates community facilities hosting social, cultural, sports and family programmes across the city's eight districts. This tender is administered by the Procurement & Contracts Department on its behalf.

### 1.2 Purpose of this Request for Proposal

MNDA invites suitably qualified facilities management (FM) companies to submit technical and commercial proposals for integrated hard and soft FM services at five community facilities for 24 months commencing 1 January 2027 (the "Contract"). The services are currently delivered under a contract expiring on 31 December 2026. MNDA seeks safe, reliable and energy-efficient operation, transparent real-time performance data, and a measurable improvement in thermal comfort and energy performance during the summer months, when cooling loads peak.

### 1.4 Al Ghaf Community Centre — spaces and principal plant

Al Ghaf Community Centre (MN-07) is the flagship facility of the Contract (GFA 14,200 m², basement, ground and first floor). Spaces include a 650-seat Multipurpose Hall, the Ladies' Fitness centre with studios and changing rooms, a public library, a majlis, children's activity rooms, training rooms and a café operated by a licensee. Principal plant:

- Two air-cooled screw chillers, CH-01 and CH-02, 350 TR each, roof-mounted, with three primary chilled-water pumps (two duty, one standby) and a variable-speed secondary circuit;
- Eight air handling units (AHU-01 to AHU-08), of which two serve the Multipurpose Hall and one serves the Ladies' Fitness centre, plus 96 fan coil units;
- Main LV switchboard, 14 sub-main distribution boards and a 500 kVA standby diesel generator with automatic transfer switch;
- Building management system (BMS) with approximately 1,150 points;
- Addressable fire alarm with voice evacuation, sprinklers, fire pump set and kitchen hood wet-chemical suppression.

### 1.5 Satellite facilities — building services summary

- **Al Murjan Community Majlis (MN-02):** 16 ducted DX split units, conventional fire alarm, domestic water tanks and booster set. Heavy evening and Ramadan use.
- **Al Waha Gardens Community Pavilion (MN-04):** VRF system (4 outdoor condensing units, 46 indoor units) with central controller, addressable fire alarm, 60 kWp rooftop solar PV array.
- **Corniche Family Park Amenity Buildings (MN-01):** three single-storey blocks with split units, booster pumps and a sewage lift station. Very high public footfall at weekends and on public holidays.
- **Ras Al Marsa Workers' Recreation Centre (MN-05):** 6 packaged rooftop units, addressable fire alarm, canteen extract system and floodlighting to the outdoor cricket ground.

## 3. Scope of services

### 3.1 Service delivery model and operating hours

The Contractor shall provide integrated hard and soft FM services as a single point of responsibility for all five facilities. A resident team shall be based at Al Ghaf Community Centre; the satellite facilities may be served by resident attendants and cleaners supported by a mobile maintenance team, provided the response times in Schedule 3 are met at every facility.

| Facility | Normal operating hours |
|---|---|
| Al Ghaf Community Centre | Daily 07:00–23:00; events until 01:00 |
| Al Murjan Community Majlis | Daily 16:00–23:00; Ramadan 20:00–02:00 |
| Al Waha Gardens Community Pavilion | Daily 08:00–22:00 |
| Corniche Family Park Amenity Buildings | Daily 06:00–24:00 |
| Ras Al Marsa Workers' Recreation Centre | Mon–Fri 15:00–23:00; Sat–Sun 08:00–23:00 |

MNDA will notify events outside normal hours at least 48 hours in advance.

### 3.2 Hard services — HVAC and chilled-water systems

The Contractor shall operate and maintain all HVAC plant, including chillers CH-01 and CH-02, chilled-water pumps, AHUs, FCUs, DX, VRF and packaged units, fans, ductwork, dampers and controls. The scope includes chemical treatment of the closed chilled-water circuit with monthly water analysis, refrigerant management by certified technicians, and design conditions in occupied spaces of 23 °C ± 1.5 °C and relative humidity not exceeding 60%. The Al Ghaf chillers operate at ambient temperatures above 46 °C in summer; condenser heat-rejection performance shall be maintained accordingly.

### 3.3 Hard services — electrical systems

The scope covers LV switchboards and distribution boards, lighting and controls, emergency lighting, the standby diesel generator (including fuel management and monthly on-load runs), automatic transfer switches, earthing and lightning protection, small power, external lighting and sports floodlighting, with an annual thermographic survey of all boards. Utility-owned substation equipment is excluded.

### 3.4 Hard services — plumbing, drainage and water systems

The scope covers domestic water tanks (cleaning and disinfection every six months), booster pumps, water heaters, sanitaryware, drainage, grease traps and the sewage lift station at the Corniche Family Park, together with a Legionella risk management programme (annual risk assessment, monthly sentinel-outlet temperature checks, weekly flushing of low-use outlets).

### 3.5 Hard services — fire and life safety systems

The scope covers fire alarm and detection, voice evacuation, sprinklers, fire pumps, hose reels, portable extinguishers, kitchen hood suppression, emergency lighting and exit signage, fire doors and smoke dampers. All maintenance, testing and certification of fire and life safety systems shall be carried out by a contractor approved by the competent Civil Defence authority. The Contractor shall keep all statutory certificates current and make test records available to MNDA and to the Civil Defence authority on request.

### 3.6 Hard services — building management system

The Contractor shall operate and maintain the BMS at Al Ghaf Community Centre and the VRF central controller at Al Waha Gardens, including alarm monitoring, trend logging at intervals not exceeding 15 minutes, time schedules aligned with operating and event hours, annual sensor calibration and monthly backups. Remote access requires MNDA's written approval.

### 3.7 Hard services — building fabric and minor works

The scope covers doors and ironmongery, ceilings, partitions, floor finishes, roofs and waterproofing (inspected before and after summer), signage and furniture repairs. Repairs including materials up to AED 2,500 per work order are included in the lump sum; above this value, works are quoted against the Form F-4 schedule of rates for MNDA approval.

### 3.8 Soft services — cleaning

The Contractor shall provide daily, periodic and deep cleaning of all internal areas, washroom hygiene services including consumables, cleaning before and after events in the Multipurpose Hall, and quarterly carpet shampooing and external glazing cleaning. Frequencies shall reflect footfall, with additional attendant cover at the Corniche Family Park on weekends and public holidays.

### 3.9 Soft services — pest control

The Contractor shall provide integrated pest management at all facilities through a municipality-approved pest control contractor, including monthly inspections and treatments, bait station mapping, bird control at the Corniche Family Park, and attendance to pest sightings in food, fitness and children's areas within P2 times.

### 3.10 Soft services — waste management

The scope covers collection from all bins, segregation at source, transfer to collection points and removal by licensed waste carriers. Hazardous waste (lamps, batteries, oily rags, recovered refrigerant) shall be handled by licensed contractors. Monthly quantities by waste stream shall be reported.

### 3.11 Helpdesk and CAFM

The Contractor shall provide a 24/7 helpdesk and a computer-aided facilities management (CAFM) system as the single system of record for all assets, PPM tasks, work orders and performance data. MNDA's existing asset register (approximately 4,860 assets) will be provided at award for migration. All data in the CAFM system is MNDA's property and shall be handed over in an open, machine-readable format at the end of the Contract.

### 3.12 Energy management

The Contractor shall monitor electricity and water consumption at all facilities using utility bills, sub-meters and BMS data, and implement energy conservation measures and plant optimisation. MNDA pays utility bills directly; energy performance is measured as set out in T-07. Al Ghaf Community Centre consumed approximately 3.18 GWh of electricity in the 12 months to August 2026, an estimated 62% of it for cooling.

### 3.13 Exclusions and interfaces

Excluded and managed by MNDA under separate contracts: passenger lifts (three at Al Ghaf, maintained by the manufacturer), landscaping and irrigation, security guarding, IT and audio-visual equipment, and the café licensee's kitchen equipment. The Contractor shall log faults on excluded systems through its helpdesk and coordinate access for other contractors.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### 5.3 Calculation of monthly SLA compliance

Monthly SLA compliance (%) = (number of PPM tasks and reactive work orders completed within their target times in the month ÷ total number of PPM tasks and reactive work orders due in the month) × 100. Work orders delayed solely by MNDA (for example, access refused by MNDA in writing) may be excluded with the approval of the MNDA Contract Administrator. Data shall be taken from the CAFM system without manual adjustment; MNDA may audit any record.

### 5.4 Service credits

For each percentage point, or part thereof, by which monthly SLA compliance falls below 95%, a service credit of 1% of the monthly fee shall be deducted from that month's invoice. For example, compliance of 92.4% gives a shortfall of 2.6 points, rounded up to 3, and a service credit of 3%. A further credit of 1% of the monthly fee applies for each statutory certificate found expired (KPI-04). Total service credits in any month are capped at 10% of the monthly fee. Service credits are a price adjustment for reduced service and do not limit MNDA's other rights under the Contract.

### 5.5 Persistent under-performance

Where monthly SLA compliance is below 90% in any two consecutive months, or below 95% in any three months within a rolling six-month period, the Contractor shall submit a remediation plan within 10 working days for MNDA's approval. Failure to submit or implement an approved remediation plan constitutes a material breach of the Contract.

## 7. Evaluation criteria and weights

### 7.1 Evaluation stages

Bids will be evaluated in four stages: (1) administrative and mandatory compliance (pass/fail against M-01 to M-08); (2) technical evaluation of Volume 1 and Volume 2 against the sub-criteria in Section 7.2, with a **technical pass mark of 70 out of 100**; (3) commercial evaluation of technically qualified bids only, including pass/fail checks of C-02 and C-03; and (4) calculation of the combined score. The overall weighting is **70% technical and 30% commercial**. Commercial envelopes of bids scoring below 70 technical points will not be opened.

### 7.2 Technical sub-criteria and weights

| No. | Sub-criterion | Requirements | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Understanding of scope and mobilisation | T-01, T-02 | 8 |
| 2 | Maintenance methodology — PPM, HVAC, reactive, asset and lifecycle | T-03, T-04, T-05, T-06, T-08, T-09 | 22 |

| 8 | Soft services and quality management | T-10, T-11 | 6 |
| 9 | Quality of the compliance matrix and presentation | All | 4 |
| | **Total** | | **100** |

### 7.3 Technical scoring scale

Each sub-criterion is scored on a 0–5 scale and weighted: sub-criterion score = weight × (score ÷ 5). 0 — not addressed; 1 — poor, major gaps or unsupported claims; 2 — weak, several requirements not met; 3 — acceptable, requirements met with adequate evidence; 4 — good, met in full with contract-specific evidence; 5 — excellent, exceeded with verifiable evidence. A score of 0 or 1 on sub-criterion 2, 5 or 6 may lead to rejection irrespective of the total technical score.

### 7.4 Commercial evaluation formula

The evaluated price is the total of Form F-4 for the 24-month term excluding VAT, corrected for arithmetic errors, plus MNDA's valuation of any priced deviation declared in Form F-2. Commercial score = (lowest evaluated price among technically qualified bids ÷ the Bidder's evaluated price) × 30. Where an evaluated price is more than 20% below the average of the technically qualified bids, MNDA may require the Bidder to justify its pricing before the score is confirmed.

### 7.5 Combined score and award recommendation

Combined score = (technical score × 0.70) + commercial score, giving a maximum of 100. The Tender Committee will recommend award to the bidder with the highest combined score. Where two bids have equal combined scores, the bid with the higher technical score ranks first. The recommendation is subject to approval in accordance with MNDA's delegation of authority.
