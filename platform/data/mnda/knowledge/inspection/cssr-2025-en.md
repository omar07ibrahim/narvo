These excerpts reproduce Parts 1 and 2, the thirteen articles of Parts 3–5 most cited in inspections, Part 6
(Enforcement) and Annex B (penalties). Articles not reproduced remain in force. Where the Arabic and English texts
differ, the Arabic text prevails.

## Part 1 — Title, Commencement and Scope (Art. 1–2)

**Art. 1.** This Regulation may be cited as CSSR-2025. It is issued under Director General Resolution No. 27 of 2025
and comes into force on 1 March 2025. Sites with an active permit on that date had until 31 May 2025 to comply with
Art. 5.1 and Art. 6.2.

**Art. 2.** It applies to all construction, demolition, shoring and external alteration works in Marsa Al Noor under
a building permit (MNDA-SVC-101), a fit-out permit (MNDA-SVC-102) involving structural or external works, or a
demolition permit, in all districts MN-01 to MN-08. In the gated communities MN-03, MN-06 and MN-08, master-developer
site rules may add to but not relax this Regulation. It binds the main contractor, subcontractors, the supervising
consultant and every person on site.

## Part 1 — Definitions (Art. 3)

- **Authorised Inspector** — an Authority employee authorised in writing by the Director, Field Operations &
  Inspection (Art. 30.1).
- **Competent person** — a person with the training, qualifications and experience to carry out a defined task safely
  and recognise its hazards, appointed in writing by the main contractor (e.g. scaffold, lifting or excavation
  supervisor). Appointment letter and certificates must be on site.
- **Open edge** — any edge of a slab, roof, balcony, stair, platform or floor opening from which a person could fall
  2 m or more, or any height into water, machinery or onto protruding reinforcement.
- **Exclusion zone** — a physically barricaded area from which everyone not directly involved in an activity is kept
  out; for lifting, at least the load path plus 3 m.
- **Corrective action** — action that permanently removes a non-compliance and its hazard. An **interim measure**
  (e.g. barricading a stair void until guardrails are fixed) does not close a finding.
- **Verification** — confirmation by an Authorised Inspector, by re-inspection or review of photographic evidence
  (Art. 31.5), that corrective action is complete and effective.
- **Working day** — Monday to Friday excluding official public holidays; Friday counts as a full working day.

## Part 2 — Duties of the Main Contractor (Art. 4–5)

**Art. 4.** The main contractor is responsible for safety on the whole site, including subcontractors' work. It shall
submit a Construction Safety Plan before mobilisation, update it at each change of phase, and induct every worker
before first entry in a language the worker understands.

## Art. 5.1 — Site Safety Officer Ratio

- (a) Sites with **25 or more workers**: at least **one full-time site safety officer (SSO) per 100 workers** or part
  thereof (214 workers require 3 SSOs). The SSO must hold a recognised safety diploma and be registered with the
  Authority.
- (b) Sites with fewer than 25 workers: a competent site supervisor appointed as safety coordinator.
- (c) One additional SSO for each shift working after 19:00; a site safety manager on sites over 500 workers.

## Art. 5.2 — Daily Site Safety Inspections by the Contractor

- (a) The SSO or a competent person inspects the site **every day before work starts**, covering open edges, scaffolds,
  excavations, temporary electrics, lifting equipment, extinguishers at hot work, access routes and housekeeping.
- (b) Each defect is recorded with location, photograph, action taken and closing time. Imminent danger stops the
  affected work immediately.
- (c) Scaffolds are inspected every 7 days, after alteration and after winds above 50 km/h; excavations at the start
  of each shift and after rain.

## Art. 5.3 — Site Safety Records

- (a) Kept at the site office: safety plan; SSO and competent-person appointments; induction register; daily
  inspection records (last 90 days); scaffold register; lifting certificates (cranes 12 months, accessories 6 months);
  lift plans; permits to work; extinguisher log; incident register; corrective-action log for every NoV.
- (b) Produced to an Authorised Inspector on request, within **2 hours** if held off site; retained **3 years** after
  the Building Completion Certificate (MNDA-SVC-103).
- (c) Serious incidents (fatality, hospital admission over 24 hours, collapse, crane failure) are reported within
  **2 hours** on 800 6632 and in writing within 24 hours.

## Part 3 — Site Establishment and Personal Protection (Art. 6–11)

Reproduced: Art. 6.2 hoarding and signage (Major) and Art. 8.1 PPE (Major).

## Art. 6.2 — Site Hoarding and Site Signage

**Class:** Major · **Timescale:** 3 working days (Art. 31.3) · **Defect code:** D06

**6.2** Sites adjoining public areas shall be enclosed by continuous, undamaged hoarding at least 2.4 m high, and
display a bilingual site board (permit number, contractor, consultant, emergency contact) and mandatory safety
signage at every gate.

- (a) Solid panels (profiled sheet or plywood) on posts designed for wind load; no gap over 50 mm; gates closed when
  not in use.
- (b) Site board at least 2.0 m × 1.5 m showing also the plot number (MN-DD-SSS-PPPP) and contractor's trade licence.
- (c) At every gate: PPE-mandatory signs, "Authorised persons only" and directions to the assembly point.

**Inspector guidance.** Photograph from the public side with the panel height for scale. Non-compliance: gaps,
fallen panels, open perimeter, missing board or signs. A manned, barriered vehicle gate is not a gap.

## Art. 8.1 — Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

**Class:** Major · **Timescale:** 3 working days (Art. 31.3) · **Defect code:** D02

**8.1** All persons on site shall wear a safety helmet, high-visibility vest or clothing, and safety footwear at all
times; eye, hearing and hand protection where the task requires; a full-body harness clipped to a rated anchor when
working at height without collective protection.

- (a) Helmets worn peak forward, chin strap fastened at height; a cloth cap under a correctly fitted helmet is allowed.
- (b) Hi-vis orange or yellow, visible front and back; boots with toe cap and midsole — sandals and trainers prohibited.
- (c) Harness anchors rated at least 12 kN with 100% tie-off; hearing protection above 85 dB(A).

**Inspector guidance.** Record persons in frame and how many are non-compliant. Site offices and welfare cabins are
outside the PPE requirement.

## Part 4 — Work at Height, Structures and Excavations (Art. 12–17)

Reproduced: Art. 12.3 edge protection (Critical), 13.6 rebar starters (Major), 15.2 scaffolding (Critical), 16.3
ladders (Major), 17.4 excavations (Critical).

## Art. 12.3 — Edge Protection at Open Edges and Floor Openings

**Class:** Critical · **Timescale:** 24 hours + area stop-work (Art. 31.2) · **Defect code:** D01

**12.3** Every open edge, slab perimeter, floor opening or stair void where a person could fall 2 m or more shall
have guardrails: top rail 950–1,100 mm, intermediate rail, and toe board of at least 150 mm, or an equivalent barrier.

- (a) No unprotected gap over 470 mm; posts at not more than 2.4 m centres; top rail resists 0.9 kN. Tape, rope or
  chain is not a guardrail.
- (b) Floor openings over 200 mm: guardrails, or a fixed cover rated for twice the expected load marked "OPENING — DO
  NOT REMOVE".
- (c) Equivalent barrier: proprietary edge system, completed parapet at least 950 mm, or guarded scaffold at the edge.
  Debris netting alone is not edge protection. Removal only under a permit, with workers clipped on, and reinstated
  before shift end.

**Inspector guidance.** Compliance shows top rail, mid-rail and toe board along the full edge, with a folding rule for
height. Check from a second viewpoint that protection is not fitted out of frame.

## Art. 13.6 — Protruding Reinforcement Bars (Rebar Starters)

**Class:** Major · **Timescale:** 3 working days (Art. 31.3) · **Defect code:** D04

**13.6** Protruding reinforcement bars (starter bars, dowels) that present an impalement or laceration hazard shall
be capped with approved mushroom caps or covered with protective troughs.

- (a) Orange or yellow caps sized for the bar (12–32 mm); impalement-rated caps with steel insert below working
  platforms.
- (b) Rows of wall starters covered by a continuous trough along the full row.
- (c) Bars enclosed in erected formwork, or in a pour area closed to other trades where concreting starts the same
  shift, are not a hazard.

**Inspector guidance.** Non-compliance: exposed bar ends near walkways or working positions.

## Art. 15.2 — Scaffolding: Boarding, Guardrails, Ties and Inspection Tags

**Class:** Critical · **Timescale:** 24 hours + area stop-work (Art. 31.2) · **Defect code:** D03

**15.2** Scaffolds shall be erected by competent persons, fully boarded at working lifts, fitted with guardrails and
toe boards, braced and tied to the structure, set on base plates and sole boards, and carry a valid scaffold
inspection tag (green) renewed every 7 days.

- (a) Platforms at least 600 mm wide, no board gap over 25 mm; guardrails 950–1,100 mm, mid-rail, 150 mm toe board.
- (b) Base plates on timber sole boards at least 225 mm wide; no blocks as packing; ties at least every 4 m vertically
  and 6 m horizontally; scaffolds over 15 m need a design calculation.
- (c) Green tag at every access point with inspection date, inspector and load class; expires 7 days after the date.
  Incomplete scaffolds carry a red "Do not use" tag with access blocked.

**Inspector guidance.** Photograph the full elevation, then the tag with the date legible. An expired tag is a finding.

## Art. 16.3 — Safe Use of Ladders

**Class:** Major · **Timescale:** 3 working days (Art. 31.3) · **Defect code:** D09

**16.3** Ladders shall be industrial grade, undamaged, set at about 75° (1:4), secured top or bottom, extend 1 m
above the landing, and used for access or short-duration light work only, maintaining three points of contact.

- (a) Industrial grade: rated at least 150 kg; fibreglass for electrical work; site-made or painted ladders prohibited.
- (b) Short-duration light work: not more than 30 minutes in one position, carrying not more than 10 kg.
- (c) No standing on the top three rungs; damaged ladders removed from site.

**Inspector guidance.** Non-compliance: makeshift or damaged ladder, unsecured, too steep or shallow, used as a platform.

## Art. 17.4 — Excavations: Support, Barriers, Spoil and Access

**Class:** Critical · **Timescale:** 24 hours + area stop-work (Art. 31.2) · **Defect code:** D07

**17.4** Excavations deeper than 1.2 m shall be shored, battered or benched; edges shall be protected by rigid
barriers set at least 1 m back from the edge; spoil and materials kept at least 1 m from the edge; safe access by
ladder or ramp provided.

- (a) Batters no steeper than 1:1 (45°) in sandy coastal soils unless the geotechnical report allows otherwise.
- (b) Rigid barriers: mesh panels or post-and-rail 1.0–1.1 m high, continuous. Tape or cones alone are not accepted.
- (c) Ladder or ramp at least every 15 m of trench; plant kept 2 m back by stop blocks.

**Inspector guidance.** Include a person, ladder or ranging pole for depth. Non-compliance: no barrier, spoil at the
edge, vertical unsupported sides, no ladder.

## Part 5 — Electrical, Fire, Access, Housekeeping and Lifting (Art. 18–22)

Reproduced: Art. 18.2 electrical (Critical), 19.2 extinguishers (Major), 20.3 egress (Major), 21.1 housekeeping
(Minor), 21.4 stacking (Major), 22.5 lifting (Critical).

## Art. 18.2 — Temporary Electrical Installations

**Class:** Critical · **Timescale:** 24 hours + area stop-work (Art. 31.2) · **Defect code:** D10

**18.2** Temporary electrical supplies shall be installed by a licensed electrician, protected by 30 mA RCDs, with
distribution boards closed and locked; cables shall be armoured or protected, routed overhead or along edges, and
kept out of water and walkways.

- (a) Tested before use and every 3 months; RCDs trip-tested weekly.
- (b) Boards at least IP44, labelled, mounted 0.5 m above ground.
- (c) Overhead cables at least 2.5 m high; taped joints, domestic extension leads and cables in water prohibited.

**Inspector guidance.** Non-compliance: open boards, cables across walkways or in water, improvised connections.

## Art. 19.2 — Fire Extinguishers at Hot Work, Fuel Stores and Generators

**Class:** Major · **Timescale:** 3 working days (Art. 31.3) · **Defect code:** D08

**19.2** A charged, inspected fire extinguisher of suitable type shall be within 10 m of every hot-work location,
fuel or gas store, generator and site office, mounted or on a stand and visibly signed.

- (a) 6 kg dry powder (ABC) for general use and hot work; 5 kg CO₂ for boards and generators.
- (b) Gauge in the green zone, current monthly tag, annual service by a licensed provider.
- (c) Hot work needs a permit and a 30-minute fire watch after work stops.

**Inspector guidance.** Use a wide shot showing both the hazard and the extinguisher so the distance can be judged.

## Art. 20.3 — Access Routes and Emergency Egress

**Class:** Major · **Timescale:** 3 working days (Art. 31.3) · **Defect code:** D12

**20.3** Designated pedestrian routes, stair cores, emergency exits and assembly-point routes shall be kept clear at
all times, at least 1.2 m wide, and separated from vehicle routes.

- (a) Physical barriers between pedestrians and vehicles where plant operates.
- (b) Stairs in use have temporary handrails and at least 50 lux lighting after dark.
- (c) Assembly point signed and shown on the site plan at every gate.

**Inspector guidance.** Non-compliance: materials in stairs, doorways or exit routes; no segregated pedestrian path.

## Art. 21.1 — Housekeeping and Daily Waste Removal

**Class:** Minor · **Timescale:** 7 working days (Art. 31.4) · **Defect code:** D05

**21.1** Work areas, walkways and stairs shall be kept free of debris, offcuts, packaging and waste; waste shall be
removed to designated skips at least daily.

- (a) Waste segregated in designated skips, removed before overflow by a licensed transporter.
- (b) Waste lowered from upper floors by chute or hoist, never thrown.
- (c) Nails removed or bent over in timber offcuts.

**Inspector guidance.** Materials staged at the work face for same-day use are not waste.

## Art. 21.4 — Material Stacking and Storage

**Class:** Major · **Timescale:** 3 working days (Art. 31.3) · **Defect code:** D11

**21.4** Materials shall be stacked on firm level ground, stable and interlocked, no higher than 2 m (or 3 times the
base width), kept at least 1 m from slab edges and excavations, and secured against wind.

- (a) Height limit: 2 m or 3 times the least base width, whichever is lower; pipes chocked.
- (b) Loads on suspended slabs within the structural engineer's limit.
- (c) Sheets and boards strapped or weighted — coastal gusts regularly exceed 40 km/h.

**Inspector guidance.** Use a person or pallet for height. Non-compliance: leaning or tall stacks, material at edges.

## Art. 22.5 — Lifting Operations and Exclusion Zones

**Class:** Critical · **Timescale:** 24 hours + area stop-work (Art. 31.2) · **Defect code:** D13

**22.5** During lifting operations the area beneath and around the suspended load shall be barricaded as an exclusion
zone, controlled by a certified banksman/signaller; no person shall stand or pass under a suspended load.

- (a) Every lift follows a lift plan; cranes certified for 12 months, accessories for 6 months.
- (b) Zone covers the load path plus 3 m, barricaded and signed "Lifting in progress — keep out".
- (c) Banksman wears a vest marked "BANKSMAN"; tag lines used; lifting stops above 50 km/h wind.

**Inspector guidance.** A load resting on the ground with slings attached is not a suspended load.

## Part 6 — Enforcement (Art. 30–32)

Part 6 sets inspection powers, severity classes and corrective timescales, and the Notice of Violation. Penalties
are in Annex B.

## Art. 30 — Inspection Powers, Photographic Evidence and AI-Assisted Image Review

- **30.1** Inspections are carried out by Authorised Inspectors holding written authorisation from the Director, Field
  Operations & Inspection.
- **30.2** An inspector may enter any site during working hours without notice, and at any time where imminent danger
  is suspected. In gated communities the master developer grants access within 15 minutes of arrival at the gate.
- **30.3** The inspector may measure, photograph and video-record, require the records in Art. 5.3, interview persons
  on site and order immediate cessation of any activity presenting imminent danger.
- **30.4 Photographic evidence.** Every finding is supported by at least one photograph showing the non-compliance in
  context, carrying date, time and location recorded by the Authority's inspection device. Originals are kept
  unaltered; annotations are made on copies.
- **30.5 AI-assisted image review.** The Authority may use AI-assisted review to screen inspection photographs:
  - (a) **AI flags are advisory only** and are not findings;
  - (b) **every finding must be confirmed by an Authorised Inspector before a Notice of Violation is issued**;
  - (c) the inspector may reject a flag, change its defect type or class, or add a defect not flagged; each decision
    is recorded with the inspector's name and time;
  - (d) no notice or penalty may rest on an AI flag alone; AI use follows the Authority's AI policies in force.
- **30.6** Refusing entry to or obstructing an inspector is an offence (Annex B.4).

## Art. 31 — Severity Classes and Corrective Timescales

- **31.1 Classification.** Each finding is **Critical**, **Major** or **Minor** as assigned in Table B.1. A higher
  class applies only under Art. 31.6, or with the Head of Site Inspection Section's written approval where imminent
  danger exists.
- **31.2 Critical — 24 hours.** Rectify **within 24 hours** of service of the notice. The **affected work area is
  stopped until the corrective action is verified**; an interim measure is required immediately.
- **31.3 Major — 3 working days.** Rectify **within 3 working days** of service.
- **31.4 Minor — 7 working days.** Rectify **within 7 working days** of service.
- **31.5 Verification of closure.** A finding closes only when an Authorised Inspector verifies the corrective action,
  by **re-inspection** or by **reviewing photographic evidence** submitted through the Authority's e-services:
  timestamped, from the same viewpoint as the original and showing the complete corrective action. Insufficient
  evidence is rejected and re-inspection required. Area stop-work is lifted only on verification.
- **31.6 Repeat violations.** A violation of the **same article, on the same site, by the same main contractor within
  90 days** of the previous notice is **escalated one class** (Minor → Major, Major → Critical). A repeat Critical
  within 90 days may lead to a site-wide stop-work order.
- **31.7 Counting.** Timescales run from service. Critical is counted in clock hours; Major and Minor in working days
  starting the working day after service and ending at 23:59 on the last day.

## Art. 32 — Notice of Violation (NoV)

- **32.1 Authority.** Notices are issued **under the authority of the Director, Field Operations & Inspection — Eng.
  Hamad Juma Al Kaabi**, and signed by the Authorised Inspector who confirmed the findings.
- **32.2 Required content.** Inspection reference (MNDA-INS-YYYY-NNNNN); date and time; plot number; building permit
  number; main contractor and trade licence number; consultant; for each finding: description, article, severity
  class, due date and time, Annex B penalty, verification method and photograph references; any stop-work order;
  right of objection; inspector's name and authorisation number.
- **32.3 Language.** Bilingual Arabic and English in one document; Arabic prevails.
- **32.4 Service.** Electronic, to the main contractor's e-services account and registered e-mail, with copies to
  owner and consultant; effective at dispatch.
- **32.5 Objection.** To the Director, Field Operations & Inspection, within **10 working days**; it does not suspend
  stop-work or timescales; decided within 15 working days.
- **32.6 Payment.** Within 30 days. Unpaid penalties block new permit applications by the contractor and the Building
  Completion Certificate (MNDA-SVC-103) for the plot.

### B.2 — Repeat offences: penalty doubled within 12 months

- B.2.1 The penalty is **doubled** where the same article was violated on the same site by the same main contractor
  within the previous **12 months**.
- B.2.2 Where Art. 31.6 also escalates the class (repeat within 90 days), the penalty is the **higher** of the
  escalated class's first-offence penalty and double the original penalty; the two are not added.

| Situation | Class applied | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Minor repeated after 45 days | Major (escalated) | AED 5,000 |
| Minor repeated after 200 days | Minor | AED 2,000 |
| Major repeated after 60 days | Critical (escalated) | AED 10,000 |
| Critical repeated after 30 days | Critical | AED 20,000 + possible site-wide stop-work |

### B.3 — Stop-work orders

- **Area stop-work** applies automatically to the affected area for every Critical finding until verification.
- A **site-wide stop-work order** may be issued by the Head of Site Inspection Section for three or more Critical
  findings in one inspection, a repeat Critical within 90 days, breach of an area stop-work, or uncontrolled imminent
  danger. It is lifted only by re-inspection on site.

### B.4 — Other offences

| Offence | Penalty |
|---|---|

| Failure to rectify by the due date | Additional penalty equal to the class penalty |

| Altered or re-used verification photographs | AED 50,000 + referral to Legal Affairs |
