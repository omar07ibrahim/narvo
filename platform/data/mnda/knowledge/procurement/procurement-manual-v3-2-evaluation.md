# MNDA Procurement Manual — Version 3.2

## Part 3 — Evaluation of Bids and Award Recommendation

**Document ref:** MNDA/PRC/MAN/3.2 · **Approved by:** H.E. Eng. Khalid Saif Al Mazrouei, Director General, on 2 March 2025 · **Owner:** Procurement & Contracts Department

This Part applies to limited and public tenders. Where a tender document sets a specific rule within the limits of this Part, the tender document prevails for that tender.

### Article 19 — Two-envelope system and bid opening

1. Tenders for services and works above AED 1,000,000 shall be submitted in two envelopes through the MNDA Supplier Portal: **Envelope A (technical)** and **Envelope B (commercial)**.
2. Envelope A is opened at the closing time by an opening committee of at least three members. The opening minutes record each bidder, the submission time and the documents received. Late bids are rejected automatically by the Portal.
3. Envelope B remains encrypted and unopened until the technical evaluation report has been approved by the committee chair. Commercial envelopes are opened only for bidders that have passed the mandatory requirements and the technical pass mark.
4. Commercial envelopes of disqualified bidders remain unopened and are retained in the Portal.

### Article 20 — Mandatory requirements (pass/fail gates)

1. Mandatory requirements stated in the tender documents are assessed on a **pass/fail** basis **before any technical scoring** begins.
2. Typical mandatory requirements include valid certifications, a valid In-Country Value certificate, required systems or capabilities (for example a CAFM system or 24/7 helpdesk), an Emiratisation plan, and acceptance of the scope without material exclusion.
3. A bidder that fails a mandatory requirement shall not be scored, unless the failure is resolved through a clarification permitted under Article 29.
4. The committee shall record the result for each bidder and each mandatory requirement, with a reference to the supporting document in the bid.

### Article 21 — Validity of mandatory certificates

1. Mandatory certificates and licences shall be **valid at the tender closing date**. A certificate that had expired at the closing date shall be treated as not provided.
2. Where a bidder submits an expired copy in error, a renewed certificate may be accepted through clarification only if the bidder demonstrates that the certificate was issued, and valid without interruption, **on or before the closing date**.
3. A certificate issued or renewed after the closing date shall not be accepted for that tender.
4. Where a mandatory certificate expires during the contract term, the bidder shall commit to maintain it, and the obligation shall be included in the contract.

### Article 22 — Technical scoring scale and independent evaluation

1. Each technical sub-criterion is scored on a scale of **0 to 10** by each voting evaluator **independently**, before any discussion among evaluators:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not addressed or unacceptable |
| 1–3 | Weak: major gaps or unsupported statements |
| 4–6 | Adequate: meets the requirement with minor gaps |
| 7–8 | Good: fully meets the requirement with clear evidence |
| 9–10 | Excellent: exceeds the requirement with specific, verifiable commitments |

2. Each score shall be accompanied by a short written justification referring to the relevant section of the bid.
3. Weighted sub-criterion scores are combined into a technical score out of 100.

### Article 23 — Moderation of technical scores

1. After independent scoring, the committee chair convenes a moderation meeting.
2. Where individual scores for a sub-criterion differ by three points or more, the evaluators concerned shall explain their reasoning, and the committee shall agree a moderated score.
3. The moderated score and the reasons for any change from the individual scores are recorded in the moderation sheet, which is signed by all voting members.
4. Moderation shall be based only on the content of the bid and permitted clarifications.

### Article 24 — Technical pass mark

The technical pass mark is **70 out of 100**, unless a different pass mark is stated in the tender documents. Bidders scoring below the pass mark are technically non-compliant and their commercial envelopes shall not be opened.

### Article 25 — Weighting of technical and commercial scores

1. For **services**, the weighting is normally **70% technical and 30% commercial**.
2. For **works**, the weighting is normally **40% technical and 60% commercial**.
3. For goods, the weighting shall be stated in the tender documents and is normally 20% technical and 80% commercial.
4. Any different weighting requires approval by the Director, Procurement & Contracts before the tender is published, and shall be stated in the tender documents.

### Article 26 — Commercial scoring formula

1. The commercial score of each technically compliant bidder is calculated as:

**Commercial score = (lowest compliant evaluated price ÷ bidder's evaluated price) × commercial weight**

2. Example: lowest compliant price AED 12,436,500; bidder's price AED 13,951,200; commercial weight 30. Commercial score = 12,436,500 ÷ 13,951,200 × 30 = **26.74**.
3. The total score is the weighted technical score plus the commercial score. Prices are compared exclusive of VAT and after any normalisation under Article 27.
4. Arithmetic errors are corrected in accordance with the tender conditions; unit rates prevail over totals.

### Article 27 — Price normalisation for omitted or excluded scope

1. Where a bid omits or excludes an item of the required scope, the committee shall, **for evaluation purposes only**, add to that bid the **highest price quoted for the equivalent item by the other compliant bidders**.
2. The normalised price is used only in the commercial formula. The contract price, if awarded, remains the price submitted by the bidder, and the omitted item shall be delivered within it or formally excluded from the contract.
3. Where the bidder confirms through a written clarification that the item is included in its submitted price at no additional cost, the committee shall decide whether the confirmation is acceptable under Article 29 and whether normalisation is still required. The decision and its reasons shall be recorded in the evaluation report.

### Article 28 — In-Country Value (ICV)

1. A valid ICV certificate is **mandatory for all tenders above AED 1,000,000**.
2. The minimum ICV score is set for each tender in the tender documents. For facilities management services the minimum is **25%**.
3. Where stated in the tender documents, ICV may be scored and may carry **up to 15% of the commercial weight**. Where it is not stated, ICV is assessed only as a pass/fail requirement.
4. The ICV score used is the score shown on the certificate valid at the closing date.

### Article 29 — Clarifications during evaluation

1. Clarification requests shall be issued **in writing through the MNDA Supplier Portal** and **approved by the committee chair** before they are sent. No oral clarification is permitted.
2. A clarification **must not change the price or the substance of the bid**, and must not give a bidder an opportunity to improve its offer in a way that is unfair to other bidders.
3. Bidders shall respond **within five working days** of the request, unless the chair allows a longer period in writing. A late or missing response is evaluated on the basis of the bid as submitted.
4. All requests and responses are recorded in the Portal and summarised in the evaluation report, with the committee's assessment of each response.

### Article 30 — AI-assisted evaluation and evaluator overrides

1. AI-assisted tools approved by the Authority may be used to check compliance, extract evidence and propose draft scores. Their outputs are **advisory**; each evaluator remains responsible for his or her own scores.
2. Any evaluator decision that departs from an AI-assisted proposed score or finding, and any change to an individual evaluator's score after moderation, shall be **justified in writing** and **logged**, stating the original value, the revised value, the reason, the evidence reference, the user and the time.
3. AI-assisted tools shall not process commercial envelopes before the technical evaluation report has been approved.
4. The override log forms part of the evaluation record and is available to Internal Audit.

### Article 31 — Evaluation report

The evaluation report shall be signed by all voting members and shall include, as a minimum:

1. the tender details, estimated value, bidders and opening minutes;
2. the results of the mandatory pass/fail assessment;
3. individual and moderated technical scores with justifications;
4. clarifications issued and responses received, with the committee's assessment;
5. any price normalisation and corrections of arithmetic errors;
6. commercial scores, ICV results and the final ranking;
7. the override log and any dissenting opinions;
8. the award recommendation and the recommended contract value.

### Article 32 — Award recommendation and approval

1. The contract is recommended for award to the technically compliant bidder with the **highest total score**, subject to the availability of budget.
2. Where the recommended bid exceeds the approved budget by more than 10%, the recommendation shall be referred to the Chief Financial Officer before approval.
3. The award is approved by the authority set out in Article 17 according to the contract value.
4. The letter of award is issued only after approval. Unsuccessful bidders are notified in writing and may request a debriefing within ten working days.
