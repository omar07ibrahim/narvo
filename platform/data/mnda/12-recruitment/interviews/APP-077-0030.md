# Technical interview — APP-077-0030

**Format:** 60-minute structured technical interview, MNDA Tower, meeting room 14-B, 13:30–14:35. Notes taken by Ahmed Khalifa Al Romaithi and agreed by the panel on 6 Oct 2026.

**Q1 (Ahmed): Your CV says you lead a team of 14 reviewers and approvals engineers. Tell us how you manage them.**
Daniel explained that 14 is the size of the wider building-regulations and approvals group across the Dubai and Abu Dhabi offices. He is the technical lead: he sets review standards and signs off difficult files. Line management — recruitment, appraisals, leave and workload allocation — sits with the two office operations managers. He has five engineers who come to him directly for technical direction. He confirmed he has not conducted a formal performance appraisal.

**Q2 (Eng. Reem): Give an example of developing a reviewer who was struggling.**
His example was a junior engineer who kept missing egress issues. He said he ended up re-doing the reviews himself "because it was faster and the client was waiting", and later moved the engineer to documentation work. When prompted, he could not describe a structured coaching plan or a follow-up. He was open that he prefers to stay hands-on.

**Q3 (Eng. Omar): Technical scenario — G+32 residential tower, podium transfer slab, assembly space on level 6 with a single stair discharge through the lobby.**
Excellent answer. Identified the occupant-load and exit-capacity problem under NFPA 101 and IBC chapter 10, the need for a second exit or an exit passageway, and the structural questions on the transfer slab (load path, punching at transfer columns, long-term deflection under ACI 318-19). Proposed a sensible comment sequence so the consultant fixes life safety before the structural redesign.

**Q4 (Eng. Omar): Circular 14/2026 — how would you cut NOC-related returns?**
Proposed his revision-control protocol from the Approvals Playbook (NOC and permit set must carry the same revision; checklist at intake). Good, practical answer; consultant-side perspective.

**Q5 (Eng. Reem): How would you make eight reviewers consistent?**
Answer centred on writing a better guidance manual. Little on calibration sessions, peer review of comments or feedback loops.

**Q6 (Ahmed): Digital permitting?**
The BIM compliance pilots were run by the firm's BIM manager; Daniel defined the egress and compartmentation rules. No e-permit configuration experience.

## Panel observations

- **C1 / C2:** confirmed very strong — precise, code-referenced technical answers.
- **C5:** strong; the Approvals Playbook is substantial written evidence.
- **C6:** weaker than the CV implies — technical lead rather than people leader; no line-management or appraisal experience; tendency to do the work rather than coach.
- **C4:** in line with the CV (rule definition on two pilots only).
