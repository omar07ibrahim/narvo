# Technical interview — APP-077-0041

**Format:** 60-minute structured technical interview, MNDA Tower, meeting room 14-B, 10:00–11:05. Notes taken by Ahmed Khalifa Al Romaithi and agreed by the panel the same afternoon. Questions follow the agreed C1–C7 interview guide.

**Q1 (Eng. Omar): Your CV says you were "involved in approvals for several residential communities". What was your actual role?**
Dana clarified that from 2019 to 2022 she was the municipality's designated focal point for the Shati Al Khor gated community (approx. 1,350 plots; master developer Najmat Al Sahil Developments PJSC). She personally led the NOC coordination: chaired a fortnightly meeting with the developer's design-control team, agreed a joint 16-item checklist, and introduced the rule that the NOC must cite the approved drawing revision. Average NOC turnaround fell from about 14 working days to about 6 working days over roughly 18 months; figures came from the municipality's monthly report and she offered to provide copies. Asked why this was not in her CV, she said she had considered it "part of the job".

**Q2 (Eng. Reem): Describe the most complex building you have reviewed.**
Lead reviewer for the 42-storey Khor Nakhlah Waterfront Residence (2023): core-and-outrigger system, four basements, wind-tunnel study. Main issue she raised: the podium transfer at level 5 had been modelled with stiffness assumptions inconsistent with ACI 318-19 for cracked sections, under-estimating transfer-girder demand. She resolved it in one technical meeting with the structural consultant rather than through repeated comment rounds; permit issued at second submission. Answer detailed and confident.

**Q3 (Eng. Omar): Scenario under Circular 14/2026 — the consultant uploads Rev C, the NOC references Rev B, and the owner is pressing. What do you do?**
Would not issue the permit on a mismatched revision. Would return with a precise comment, but also call the developer to establish whether the change between B and C affects façade, roof or boundary items. If not, she would accept a short confirmation letter instead of a full new NOC — provided this is written into a department procedure, not handled case by case. Panel: practical and risk-aware.

**Q4 (Eng. Reem): Experience with e-permit platforms and digital tools?**
Limited. Khor Nakhlah moved to an e-permit platform only in early 2025; she uses it as a reviewer but has not configured workflows, rules or dashboards. Candid that she would need support from the Digital Transformation Office. Data work limited to the 2022 return-reason analysis in Excel.

**Q5 (Ahmed): How do you lead and develop reviewers?**
Leads a unit of 4; fortnightly case review; reads every comment letter before issue. Gave a concrete example of coaching a reviewer whose comments were too long and unclear, using before/after examples.

## Panel observations

- **C3:** evidence considerably stronger than the CV suggests — personally led master-developer NOC coordination for three years with a measured turnaround improvement (≈14 → ≈6 working days).
- **C7:** confirmed — lead reviewer on a 42-storey tower.
- **C4:** weaker — user-level e-permit experience only.
- **C5 / C6:** clear, structured answers; credible coaching example.
